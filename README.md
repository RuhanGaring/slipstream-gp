# Slipstream Grand Prix

A 3D Formula-style racing game for the browser. Original teams, drivers, cars and circuits. No build step. The original 2D top-down version is kept in `../f1-racing-2d/`, and this build falls back to the 2D renderer when WebGL is unavailable.

## Run it

Double-click `index.html`, or serve the folder:

```
python -m http.server 8931 --directory "C:/Users/My PC/Pictures/CLAUDE CODE/f1-racing"
```

Then open http://localhost:8931. There is also an `f1-racing` entry in the parent `.claude/launch.json`.

## Modes

- **Arcade**: drive with W A S D only. Automatic gears, traction and stability help, extra grip, a full racing line that turns red where you need to brake, no tyre wear, damage, pits or penalties. R resets the car.
- **Simulation**: tyre temperature and wear, soft, medium and hard compounds, fuel burn, front wing damage, track limits strikes and penalties, DRS (Space), pit strategy (P, then 1, 2 or 3) with a pit-lane autopilot. ABS, traction control, gearbox (E and Q for manual) and steering assist are options.
- **Quick Race**, **Championship** (5 rounds, points to P10 plus fastest lap) and **Time Trial** (ghost of your best lap, saved per circuit).
- **Spectate**: an all-AI race to watch, with a TV director that cuts between battles and cameras.
- **Replays** of every session (race, weekend sessions, time trial, spectating): from the pause menu or the results. Timeline with lap and event markers (overtakes, contacts, pit stops, fastest laps, safety car), event list, highlights, slow motion and rewind, frame stepping, any car, any camera, and photos.

## Controls

W or Up: throttle. S or Down: brake, and reverse when stopped. A and D: steer. Space: DRS. P: pit strategy. E and Q: shift. C: camera (chase, far chase, T-cam, cockpit, TV). Z: chase distance (near, standard, far). Right click: mouse steering on or off (the mouse turns a virtual wheel; tune sensitivity, deadzone, curve, self-centring, smoothing and invert in Settings). H: hide HUD. M: mute. Esc: pause. Gamepads work too.

Replays and spectating: Left and Right (or , and .) change car, Up and Down jump to the leader or the last car, C and V cycle 14 cameras (chase, far chase, T-cam, cockpit, helmet, nose, sidepod, TV, helicopter, blimp, top down, map, orbit, free), 1 to 0 pick one directly, F free camera (W A S D, Q and E for height, drag or arrows to look, Shift for speed, wheel to zoom), T top down, G map, D director, Tab timing tower, H hide the overlay. In a replay: Space play or pause, J and L skip 5 s, U and I step a frame, [ and ] speed (down into rewind), N and B next or previous event, E event list, Y highlights, P photo, Home and End, or click and drag the timeline. Esc leaves.

## Under the hood

- `js/car.js`: the vehicle model. A planar chassis carried on a vertical model over a 3D road. Each wheel has its own spin speed and inertia, driven and braked by torques and solved implicitly; tyre forces come from slip ratio and slip angle through a combined-slip magic formula, so wheelspin and lockups build progressively and braking, traction and cornering share one grip budget. Springs, dampers and anti-roll bars at each corner produce the wheel loads, so weight transfer has its real lag and kerbs, bumps, crests and compressions unsettle or press the car. Ground-effect downforce depends on ride height (with a floor stall and plank bottoming), aero balance moves with pitch, and dirty air costs the front more than the rear. The 2026 power unit (about 400 kW combustion plus a 350 kW MGU-K) runs off a battery with metered deployment, a taper at top speed, harvesting under braking and lift, and super-clipping when flat. Active aero switches to low-drag X-mode on the straights, and Overtake mode (SPACE, within a second at detection) opens up deployment. A clutch-type limited-slip differential, carbon brakes with temperature (cold or overheated discs lose bite), brake-by-wire blending regen into the rear braking, tyre surface and core temperatures, pressures, camber that changes with roll, and grip that builds as rubber goes down on the line round it out. Gamepads get rumble from kerbs, bottoming, impacts, lockups and wheelspin.
- `js/tracks.js` topography: every venue has a terrain height field and the circuit is graded over it (Lucerne climbs 30 m from the harbour, Hakone is hilly, Rimal is flat), with banked corners, raised kerb profiles with ridges, and surface roughness by type.
- `js/tracks.js`: circuits authored as corner vertices with radii, then smoothed. Kerbs, gravel and tarmac run-off, barriers, pit lane, grid, sectors and DRS zones are all derived from the centreline.
- `js/r3-grass.js`: grass blades on the verges and fields of parkland venues (procedural ones too). One instanced mesh of clumps follows the camera on the GPU; each clump is placed from a hash of its world position, set on the ground from the terrain height grid, and kept only where the circuit's grass map allows (asphalt, run-off, barriers, pit lane and every building footprint are cut out of it).
- Venue buildings (render3d.js `buildVenue`): corrugated-clad garages, a glass curtain-wall pit building, office and glass city blocks, plus paddock buildings behind the garages, a race-control tower and a sponsor footbridge over the start straight, all placed from the circuit's geometry and only where they stay clear of the track and other buildings. Facade textures are CC0 ambientCG sets embedded by `python tools/assets_venue.py DOWNLOAD_DIR` into js/assets/venue.js.
- Cockpit (r3-car.js `buildCockpit`): the eye sits under the halo's top bar so the bar frames the view and the centre pillar runs to the nose; a modern wheel about 40 cm ahead with grips, buttons, rotaries and a live screen (gear, speed, lap, position, shift lights, energy mode, battery, tyre temperatures); gloves wrap the grips and the arms follow the wheel by IK. The livery shader keeps the cockpit tub and the halo bare carbon.
- `js/trackgen.js`: procedural circuits. A seed becomes a track definition in the same form as the hand-made ones (corner vertices, width, style, pit, terrain, and a borrowed venue look for sky and scenery), so the whole pipeline runs unchanged. Layouts are star-shaped outlines with bites, infield hairpin loops, chicanes, esses and kinks, and are re-rolled until they pass the checks: length, no part of the track within run-off distance of another, pit lane clearance, a straight start/finish with room for the grid, at least one overtaking straight, and a mix of slow and fast corners. The same seed always builds the same circuit (id `gen-<seed>`); pick one from Quick Race or Time Trial, Procedural Circuit. The AI learns each generated circuit in the background (about 12 to 20 seconds), starting as soon as a seed is picked; the loading screen waits for it if needed, and the result is stored per seed in the browser so a circuit is only learned once.
- `js/learncore.js` + `js/learnjob.js`: the parallel version of the two offline tools, used for generated circuits. Stage 1 finds the racing line with a batch search: every thread tries its own bumps against the same line, the best non-overlapping improvements are kept, and every thread applies them. Stage 2 learns the grip limits with a field of test cars, each driving the same lap at a different grip level; each round narrows every corner's window between the highest clean level and the lowest failing one, then check laps with every car back off wherever any of them gets into trouble. A clean pass right after a slide or a recovery proves nothing (the car arrives slow), and test cars take no damage (a broken wing would make them slow forever). The browser runs it in Web Workers built from the sim modules already on the page (each registers itself in `F1simMods`), so it also works from file://; without workers it runs on the page in time slices.
- `js/raceline.js`: corner analysis, the driver's line (turn in from the outside, late apex before long straights, run out to the exit kerb, apex to apex through linked corners), the quasi-steady-state speed profile, and the brake / turn-in / apex / track-out reference points shown on the racing-line assist.
- `js/lines.js` (generated by `node tools/optline.js all`): the minimum-time racing line per circuit, refined from the driver's line by keeping any smooth change that lowers lap time.
- `js/ai.js`: pure-pursuit drivers with real pedal technique (stay flat until the braking demand reaches the car's capacity, brake hard in a straight line, trail off into the apex, squeeze the throttle on while the car is still loaded, lift when the rear steps out), allowance for fuel load, worn or cold tyres and being off line (including a lane that skips the swing out before a corner, which arrives on a tighter radius); an understeer response (no more throttle with the fronts saturated, and a touch of brake to tighten the line when the edge is close); brake release when the rear steps out; progressive moves across the track instead of 10 m swerves at speed; and a follow check that looks at where their own line will put them, not just where they are now; they attack (choosing the inside of the next corner), defend once before a braking zone, leave a car's width to anyone alongside, scale their pace to tyre condition and damage, and drive the pit lane.
- `js/limits.js` (generated by `node tools/learn.js all`): each circuit's real limit, learned by the AI. A reference car runs hot laps, pushes every corner and braking zone harder until it slides, runs wide, breaks track limits or touches a wall, then backs off there (bisection). Difficulty levels are fractions of this limit: Legend drives at it. A slide only counts as a failure when it is held for more than a tenth of a second, so a caught kerb twitch does not make the AI (and the guide line) brake for a flat-out corner. Re-run the tool after any physics or track change.
- Strategy (`js/race.js`): a total-race-time planner picks the start tyre and a 0, 1 or 2 stop plan from compound pace, wear and its cliff, and pit-lane loss; it re-plans each lap from measured wear, covers undercuts and saves tyres. In Simulation races of 5+ laps both compounds must be used (30 s penalty otherwise). Your race engineer uses the same planner (shown in the pit panel and called on the radio).
- `js/race.js`: lights-out start, timing, gaps, positions, collisions, penalties, pit service, classification.
- `js/render3d.js`: the 3D renderer (three.js r158, vendored in `js/vendor/`). It builds each circuit from the same centreline data the simulation uses: asphalt, rubbered line, raised kerbs, gravel and tarmac run-off, concrete walls with sponsor hoardings, armco, tyre walls, catch fences, pit lane and garages, grandstands, start gantry with working lights, braking boards, city blocks and harbour, instanced card trees and a horizon backdrop. It also handles a photo sky per venue (the sun direction and fog colour are read from the panorama), image-based lighting, soft shadows that follow the action, smoke, sparks, skid marks and the 3D racing-line assist, and the cameras: chase, far chase, T-cam, cockpit (halo view) and a TV director with trackside cameras and line-of-sight checks.
- Materials are physically based throughout. Every ground and wall surface uses a photo PBR set (albedo, normal, and a packed AO / roughness / metalness map), with world-space macro noise so the tiles never visibly repeat. Galvanised armco and steel are metallic; paint, plaster, concrete, rubber and carbon are dielectric. Glass and water are near-mirror dielectrics (water IOR 1.33). Leaves use waxy roughness with a little emission for translucency. The car has clear-coated carbon twill, a satin or gloss livery per team, rough slick rubber with moulded sidewall lettering, metal rims and an iridescent visor.
- Lighting is physically scaled. Each venue has a calibrated HDR light probe (Poly Haven, CC0) made by `tools/hdr_prep.py`: the sun disc is cut out of the probe and becomes the shadow-casting directional light with its measured irradiance and colour, the sky is normalised so a white ground plane reads 1.0, and the ground and surroundings are filled in below the horizon so glossy paint reflects them rather than open sky. The 4k photo backdrop is shown at the same radiance as the light it casts.
- `js/r3-shaders.js`: the shader system. Every lit material goes through one patcher that adds exponential height fog with a forward-scattering sun lobe and world-space macro variation, all on shared uniforms.
- `js/r3-post.js`: the HDR post stack. The scene renders into a multisampled half-float target, then SSAO (from the log depth buffer, depth-aware blur), bloom (soft threshold, 6-level chain), screen-space sun shafts, camera motion blur (your own car stays sharp), and a composite with AgX (punchy look) or ACES tone mapping, vignette, chromatic aberration and grain. Settings > Graphics has Low / Medium / High / Ultra presets plus every option on its own: post-processing, render scale (50 to 150 percent), MSAA, shadows, AO, bloom, sun shafts, motion blur, lens effects, tone mapping and exposure.
- The cars are nine 2026 models (by Dave Love SketchFab, CC BY 4.0, see `CREDITS.md`), one per team (Tessera runs the FW48 in its own colours), converted by `tools/car_prep26.mjs` (into `assets-src/cars/`) and packed by `tools/car_pack.py` into script files (`js/assets/cars/ID.js`, textures in `tex.js`) so they load from file:// too, then decoded by `js/carmodels.js`. Each keeps its own materials and textures. Two levels of detail (about 130k triangles near the camera, 40k beyond 50 m) and a one-mesh far level with the textures baked into vertex colours. Wheels spin and steer; tyres carry compound stripes. Phones load every texture at half size.
- Offline play: `tools/stamp.py` writes `assets/manifest.json` (every file the game loads, with size and hash). On boot, `js/offline.js` downloads whatever is missing or changed into Cache Storage, showing megabytes done out of the total, and `sw.js` serves every file from that cache, so after the first visit the game runs with no network.
- Cockpit and driver: each car's own steering wheel (from its model, with its buttons and materials) sits where the driver's hands are and turns with the steering at about 4:1; its screen shows the game's live dash display, and the driver's arms follow it with two-bone IK so the black gloves stay on that wheel's grips (the grip span is measured per car). The driver wears a team-coloured suit and a helmet with an iridium visor. The T-cam sits on each model's own T-cam pod, bolted to the chassis, so braking, kerbs and speed never shift the frame. (The code-built wheel and T-cam pod remain for the code-modelled car.)
- `js/r3-car.js`: the car builder (and the older code-modelled car, used if the model asset is missing) from lofted cross-sections (monocoque, sidepods, airbox, floor, front and rear wings with a working DRS flap, halo, suspension, rain light, compound-coloured tyres) in each team's livery.
- `js/r3-textures.js`, `js/assets/`: photo textures and skies. These are CC0 assets from Poly Haven (asphalt, gravel, concrete, sand, paving, sky panoramas, leaf atlases) and ambientCG (grass). `tools/assets_prep.py` resizes them, composes the foliage cards from the leaf atlases and embeds everything as data URIs so the game still runs from file://. Procedural fallbacks are used if the asset files are missing.
- `js/render.js`, `js/sprites.js`: the 2D renderer, still used for the minimap, scenery placement and the no-WebGL fallback.
- `js/audio.js`: all sound synthesised in WebAudio (V6 engine, opponents with Doppler, tyres, kerbs, impacts, menu music).

## Tests

- `node tools/simrace.js <track|all> <laps> <difficulty 0-4> <arcade|sim>`: full 20-car AI race, reports incidents and lap times.
- `node tools/optline.js [track|all]`: re-optimise the racing lines, then relearn the limits.
- `node tools/learn.js [track|all] [laps]`: relearn the AI's circuit limits.
- `node tools/straighten.js [track|all]`: run the racing-line straightening pass (`Raceline.straighten`) over the stored lines, then relearn the limits. The lap-time model treats curves gentler than about 200 m radius as free and has no yaw inertia, so the random-bump optimisers leave ripples on straights and sudden flicks across the track; the pass smooths the lap, irons out ripples and spreads flicks over a longer run. Both optimisers run it automatically.
- `node tools/learnpar.js <track|gen-SEED> [threads]`: learn a circuit with the parallel learner in Node worker threads (`WRITE=1` stores the result in js/lines.js and js/limits.js).
- `node tools/gentest.js [count] [firstSeed]`: roll procedural circuits and print their stats. Any tool also accepts a generated id such as `gen-48213`.
- `bash tools/batch.sh LAPS DIFF MODE`: 15 races (5 circuits x 3 seeds), totals of recoveries, wall hits and contacts.
- `node tools/temps.js <track> <laps>`: tyre temperatures per lap on a solo stint.
- `node tools/pacecurve.js <track> <arcade|sim>`: solo lap time at several AI paces.
- `node tools/solo.js <track> <secs> <pace>`: one AI car, line-following error.
- `node tools/keybot.js <track> <arcade|sim>`: drives with on/off W A S D only, through the same input ramps as the game.
- `python tools/play.py OUTDIR step...`: plays the real game in GPU Chromium and takes screenshots (see the file header).
